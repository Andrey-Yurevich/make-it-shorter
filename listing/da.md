# Danish (da)

## Name
Make It Shorter — Make It Shorter (brand, not translated)
not a priority market

## Short description
Lad AI forkorte sætninger og opsummere hele tekster. Indsæt dit indhold, så klarer ét klik resten – på alle sprog.
Let AI shorten sentences and summarize whole texts. Paste your content, and one click does the rest – in any language.

## Blind read
- Short description: "Sæt dem ind" ("put them in", with an unclear "them") became "Indsæt dit indhold" ("paste your content").
- Intro, steps and lists: several word-order and idiom fixes ("tid til det", "uden at der er lagt noget til", "i værktøjslinjen … åbnes", "følg med, mens …"). "Intet er digtet" read as poetry, so it became "Intet er opdigtet".
- The verb is now "Klik på Shorten" everywhere instead of a mix of "tryk"/"klik".
- Study use: "seminaret" became "undervisningen", the usual word for class in Denmark.
- Work use: "make it short and to the point" became "skær den ned til det væsentlige" ("cut it down to the essentials").
- Research use: the keyword-stuffed line became "get a first overview of a reading list by summarizing the articles – also when they are about AI".
- Copy paragraph: "pastes neatly" is now said as "looks neat when you paste it".
- Caveat paragraph: reworded idioms ("værd at bruge tyve minutter på", "tjek kilden", "gå tabt" for "slip").
- The 📋 heading is now the standard Danish "Ofte stillede spørgsmål" (FAQ).
- Misspelling answer: it now ends with "you've landed in the right place" instead of a literal "this is the tool you were looking for". The question uses the common Danish misspelling "opsumere" (one m).
- Draft answer: it now says "like text from any page", because a draft is not a page.
- Closing line: tightened, with the same three points kept.

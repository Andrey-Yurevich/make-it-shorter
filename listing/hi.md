# Hindi (hi)

## Name
Make It Shorter — Make It Shorter (brand, unchanged)
Not a priority market (Semrush database `none`, no keyword research). Wording comes from common Indian usage: „सारांश“ (summary) for summarize/summarizer, „छोटा करना“ (make short) for shorten. Everyday English loans that Indians use in Hindi stay in Devanagari: टेक्स्ट, पेस्ट, सेलेक्ट. „AI“ stays in Latin letters.

## Short description
कोई भी टेक्स्ट पेस्ट करें, AI बस एक क्लिक से उसे छोटा कर देगा। वाक्य हो या पूरा लेख, सारांश मनचाही भाषा में पाएँ।
Paste any text and the AI will shorten it in just one click. Whether it is a sentence or a whole article, get the summary in whatever language you like.

## Blind read
- Selecting text: „चुनना“ (choose) → „सेलेक्ट करना“, the word people actually use for highlighting. „चुनें“ stays for the language and tone pickers.
- "Pull in the whole page again" read like a browser refresh → "bring the page's full text back into the panel".
- "Two settings you choose once", the sections item ("loses its parts", a calque) and "a long article shrinks to its key ideas" rephrased.
- "No separate translator": „अनुवादक“ means a human translator → "no need to translate separately".
- Uses: "seminar" → "class" (Indian students don't have seminars); "straight to the point" → „सीधे मुद्दे पर“; the reading-list and "keep the one" calques rewritten.
- Caution paragraph: "which part holds the answer" → "where the part you need is"; "when a detail decides something" → "where even a small thing matters"; "AI is behind it" (sounds conspiratorial) → "it runs on AI".
- Daily limit: „अनुरोध“ (official-sounding "requests") → "daily usage".
- FAQ: "much shorter", "very short", "your choice is kept" (now "the extension remembers"), "words, not pictures of them" and "not a retelling" rephrased; "you are ready" → "that's all".
- Misspelling FAQ dropped: Hindi has no common misspelling of „सारांश“ (the variant „सारान्श“ is a valid spelling), and the reader saw the question as SEO stuffing.
- Closing line: "far shorter" left hanging ("than what?") → "a lot shorter or as short as possible".
- Declined: dropping "way shorter" from the closing line (that one is a keyword).

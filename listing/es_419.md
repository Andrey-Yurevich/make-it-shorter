## Name
Make It Shorter — Make It Shorter
not a priority market

## Short description
Acorta una frase o resume todo un texto con IA. Pega lo que quieras leer y obtén una versión breve en un clic, en cualquier idioma.
Shorten a sentence or summarize a whole text with AI. Paste whatever you want to read and get a brief version in one click, in any language.

## Blind read
- Short description: "the brief version" → "a brief version", which sounds more natural.
- Opening: the calque "come back as the same text" became "you give it… and it gives you back the same text". "With nothing added" was reworded to the idiomatic verb form.
- "Nothing made up: only what the source says": added the verb that was missing ("only uses").
- "Whether you need to shorten it a bit or a lot": a heavy construction replaced with a lighter idiomatic one.
- "Any of 57 languages" → "any of the 57 available languages".
- Uses header: a stiff "everyday uses" became the idiom for "day to day".
- Study item: "seminar" reads as "conference" in LatAm, so it is now "class". A vague verb was replaced with "summarize it really short".
- AI papers item: reworded as "even for summarizing publications on AI". The reader wanted it cut, but it is kept because it is in the source.
- Copying: "pastes with good formatting" → "keeps its formatting when you paste it".
- Summary paragraph: "worth twenty minutes of reading" → "worth spending twenty minutes on". "Go back to the source" → "check the source". The "under the hood" calque → "it runs on AI".
- Limits: "per time", a Río de la Plata regionalism, → the neutral "at a time". "Chrome's own pages" → "Chrome's internal pages", with the article added before Chrome Web Store. "There" → "in those cases".
- Images answer: "reads the words, not pictures of them" → "reads the page's text, not text inside images".
- "Make it shorter please" answer: reworded to "does exactly what you ask when you say 'shorten it, please'".
- Misspelling question: the English "summerize" became "resúmen", a very common misplaced accent on "resumen" (summary). The reader suggested cutting it. It stays, reworded to sound less like an ad ("Did I land in the right place?" / "this is the tool you were looking for").
- Closing line: the redundant "much shorter, as short as possible" was rebuilt into one flowing sentence.

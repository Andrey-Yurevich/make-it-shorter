# Finnish (fi)

## Name
Make It Shorter — Make It Shorter (brand, not translated)
not a priority market

## Short description
Tiivistä kokonaisia tekstejä ja lyhennä lauseita tekoälyllä millä kielellä tahansa. Liitä sisältö – yksi klikkaus riittää.
Summarize whole texts and shorten sentences with AI, in any language. Paste your content – one click is enough.

## Blind read
- Short description: the objects moved to the partitive ("kokonaisia tekstejä"), because the accusative read as "those particular texts". "Yksittäisiä" (single) was dropped later to fit 132 characters.
- Intro: the calque "palautuu" ("comes back") became "saat … takaisin" ("you get … back").
- Step 4: the result now "is written onto the screen" instead of the stiffer "is written into view".
- "How much shorter" paragraph: the sentences about long articles and short paragraphs were reworded natively, with the same meaning.
- Language setting: "any of 57 languages" became "in 57 different languages", and "kääntäjä" (which reads as a human translator) became "käännöstyökalu" (translation tool).
- Study use: "really short for your notes" became "into notes in a very short form".
- Research use: the tacked-on "AI papers" became "even when the topic is AI research".
- Writing use: the editor's question now uses the colloquial "Saisitko tätä vähän lyhennettyä?".
- Caveat paragraph: "worth twenty minutes" became "worth spending twenty minutes on". "A number in a contract" became "a sum written into a contract", because "luku" read as "chapter". "Slip" became "get lost along the way".
- PDF limit: it now says files opened in the built-in PDF viewer cannot be read, not the viewer itself.
- FAQ: several grammar fixes ("lyhentääkö se", matching cases in the retelling answer, the Simplified answer split into two sentences). The chat question is now "Saanko … mahtuu chat-viestiin?", because the colloquial "Saisko" looked like a typo. "Make it shorter please" became "what you ask for when you say 'shorten this'".
- Not taken: the reader called the misspelling question SEO-like. It stays, since typing without ä/ö ("tiivistelma") is a real Finnish search habit and the English line is the same kind of SEO line.
- Closing line: "commit to reading" became "decide to read", and the two "much shorter / as short as possible" phrases were merged into one smoother clause.
